## 2. Mr.Tableman 
### Daniel Duarte 

<br>

**Target userbase:**
- Anyone, with a phone, that needs to register information 

<br>

**Vision:**
- An application that allows the user to create and populate tables in an incremental manner and, after that, calculate statistics/relevant contextual information. 

<br>

**Page example:**
- A page with a series of boxes related to created tables and a "+" button bellow them. While the plus button should redirect the user to a "table creation" page, the boxes must have fillable fields related to table rows and an "export" button. The latter should generate a PDF with all the info accumulated in (and calculated from) the respective table. 

<br>

**Five (more or less) feature ideas:**
- Connection with printing services. 
- Ability to calculate general information from the table as a whole (mean, standard deviation or any specified formula)
- Ability to generate extra "secondary columns" with derived information 
- Ability to visualize columns as bar graphs, point plots, connected dots, and other potentially relevant kinds of representation. 
- Ability to compare data (columns) with predefined patters, such as bell curves and poisson distributions (potentially overlaying graphical representations).

## 5. Language QuizDuel Application 
### Paul Thiel

**Target userbase:**
- People trying to learn a language, mainly university students and young adults at beginner to intermediate level (A1-B2)
- Learners who want short, low-pressure practice sessions, ideally with friends or classmates

<br>

**Vision:**
- An application that, like Quizduel, hosts quizzes engolfing small grammar and vocabulary questions. Through these quizzes the application would allow different "players" to "compete" to see who gets the most questions right.
- Each match consists of a few short rounds. The player with the most correct answers wins and earns rating points, which makes everyday practice feel like a game.

<br>

**Page example:**
- Similar to Quizduell

<br>

**Five (more or less) feature ideas:**
- **Asynchronous 1v1 duels:** challenge a friend or a random opponent and play your rounds in turns, so nobody has to be online at the same time
- **Question categories and levels:** vocabulary, grammar, conjugation and fill-in-the-blank, tagged by CEFR level, with matchmaking by level
- **Ranking and progression:** Elo-style rating, XP, daily streaks and weekly leagues
- **Mistake review mode:** wrong answers are saved to a personal list for solo practice with spaced repetition
- **User-generated question packs:** teachers or learners create and share question sets (for example "Chapter 4 of our course"), with basic moderation
- **(Bonus) Class tournaments:** live multiplayer rooms with a join code

## 6. Graphics Calculator 
### Daniel Duarte 

<br>

**Target userbase:**
- People trying to learn math 

<br>

**Vision:**
- An application that, mimicking Geogebra, displays mathematical functions in carthesian referentials. 

<br>

**Page Example:**
- Geogebra's main page, with a carthesian referential at the center, an equation input box bellow, and a function/parameter nav bar to the left.  

<br>

**Five (more or less) feature ideas:**
- Supporting derivatives as a tangent line that slides across a function based on a parameter. 
- Supporting Complex Functions as a vertical line intercepting two "3D" functions (or, at least, 2D projections of them) overlayed (the real and the imaginary part)
- Supporting "infinite sums", as finite sums with a really big, and controllable, N number. 
- Supporting 3D functions as a whole. 
- Supporting animations based on varying parameters (for higher dimentions)

<br>

**(Optional) relevant extra information:**
- Being a clone may be cheap, but it may make our lives easier creative-wise, while still fulfilling the project's objective: to prove we have the necessary skills to take on a software project. If we really want innovation, we can always come up with creative features.
